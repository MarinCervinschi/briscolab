# Briscolab — Specification v1

A lab for Briscola bots, with a site to play against them. The core is the research: one rules engine, an arena
that pits bots against each other with honest statistics, search bots and then reinforcement learning. The site
is the showcase: play the best bots on your phone or your computer, and see how they think.

Decided on 2026-10-28 in an interview (decision 11 revised and 17 added the same day), starting from the university project
[MarinCervinschi/Briscola](https://github.com/MarinCervinschi/Briscola) (JavaFX), which stays as it is.

## Decisions

| 1   | Purpose          | **Research + showcase**: bots and arena first, a polished playable site second                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Portfolio and write-ups come from the lab's results; the site makes them playable                                                                              |
| 2   | Engine           | **Rust**, one crate of rules shared by everything: compiled to WebAssembly for the site, bound to Python (PyO3) for the lab                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | One engine means bots are trained on exactly the rules people play; fast enough for ISMCTS and millions of games                                               |
| 3   | Repository       | **Monorepo** `MarinCervinschi/briscolab`, public, MIT: `engine/` (Cargo workspace), `lab/` (Python, uv), `web/` (pnpm), `docs/`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | A rules change lands everywhere in one commit                                                                                                                  |
| 4   | Game             | **Two-player Briscola only**, standard rules ([docs/rules.md](docs/rules.md)), one game to 120 points. The engine is not hard-wired to two players, but four players and Briscola chiamata are out of v1                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | The smallest game that is still interesting; four players doubles search and RL complexity                                                                     |
| 5   | Information      | The engine exposes `observation(player)`: own hand, table, cards played, briscola, cards left. Bots never see the full state                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | No bot cheats by accident, and determinisation (PIMC, ISMCTS) samples from exactly what a player knows                                                         |
| 6   | Bots in v1       | Random, Greedy, **Hard** (the Java project's rules, without its probability estimate), exact **endgame solver** (last three tricks), **PIMC**, **ISMCTS**. All behind one interface, `act(observation) -> card`. **PIMC** plays out each sampled deal with Hard, and with the exact solver once the deck is empty. **ISMCTS** is single-observer, with UCB1 (exploration 0.1, tuned in the arena) and the same playouts; 1000 iterations by default                                                                                                                   | Strong baselines before learning: in RL-InventorySystem a classical rule beat DQN and PPO, so learning has to earn its place                                   |
| 7   | Reinforcement    | v1.1: **PPO in self-play**, PyTorch written CleanRL-style (one readable file), opponent pool, action masking, on a **PettingZoo** (AEC) environment over the Rust engine. It ships only if it beats the arena's champion                                                                                                                                                                                                             | More control than Stable-Baselines3 over self-play and masking                                                                                                 |
| 8   | Arena            | **Duplicate deals** (same shuffle, seats swapped), fixed seeds; per pair win rate and point difference with 95% intervals; **Bradley-Terry/Elo** rating from a round robin. A bot replaces the champion only if the interval excludes 50%. Search bots get a fixed number of iterations or determinisations, so a result is reproducible from its seeds. Deals run in parallel in `engine/py` (rayon); `core` stays single-threaded | Luck weighs a lot in Briscola; duplicates cut the variance, intervals keep us honest; parallel deals keep 10 000 deals per pair affordable for the search bots |
| 9   | Experiments      | **MLflow** on the maintainer's server, tailnet only, shared with the other lab projects: Postgres for runs, a private Garage bucket for artifacts (backed up to R2), Model Registry with `champion` / `candidate` aliases. Git holds code, configs and the champion models (ONNX)                                                                                                                                                                           | Runs and checkpoints never live only on one laptop, which is how RL-InventorySystem lost its models                                                            |
| 10  | Compute          | The Mac (M4): CPU for arena and search, MPS for the networks. A rented VM on the tailnet only if an experiment outgrows it. Nothing heavy on the server                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Briscola is small; the server serves TriviaMore and Zuppapp                                                                                                         |
| 11  | Site             | **TanStack Start** (Vite, React 19, TanStack Router) with static prerendering, Tailwind 4, `motion`, TanStack Query where useful, Vitest. The build is static files: the game runs client-only, the Lab pages are prerendered. PWA (installable, offline). Designed for both phone (portrait, one hand) and desktop (mouse and keyboard, wide screen); neither is a stretched version of the other                                  | Still no server. Prerendering makes the Lab articles real HTML for search engines and link previews, and Fumadocs supports Start                               |
| 12  | Bots on the site | They run **in the browser**: the engine in WebAssembly inside a **Web Worker**, networks via ONNX. Each level plays with the iteration count the arena measured (Expert: ISMCTS at 1000), with a cap of about 1 s per move in case a phone is slow. The bot shows its thinking for at least about 1.2 s, which a tap or a key skips; on phones the animations are off by default. Both numbers get tuned on a real phone                                                                                                                                                                     | Zero inference servers; the bot on the site is the one the arena measured; on a slow phone it gets a little weaker, not slower; the UI stays smooth                                                            |
| 13  | Data             | **Nothing stored by us.** Umami (no cookies) counts page views and, at the end of a game, one anonymous event (bot, engine version, difficulty, winner, point difference, whether the bot's thoughts were revealed). No moves, no database. Page views added on 2026-11-01                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Visits and win rate against real people for free, with no backend and no personal data                                                                         |
| 14  | Hosting          | **Coolify**, a static container built from `main` with the repo's `Dockerfile`, at `briscolab.cervinschi.site` (the wildcard exists). Its Terraform lives in a private infra repo. Production and local only                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Same infra as the other apps; servers and secrets stay private                                                                                                 |
| 15  | Cards            | Traditional **Neapolitan** designs from a freely licensed set: the Naples deck on Wikimedia Commons (public domain, by Trocche100), credited file by file. Our own backs, chosen in the settings (`DESIGN.md`)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Commercial decks (e.g. Modiano) are protected; Neapolitan cards are what most players expect                                                                   |
| 16  | Languages        | Site in **Italian** by default and **English**, strings in message files from day one. Repo, docs and code in **English**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | The game is Italian, the research audience is not                                                                                                              |
| 17  | Lab pages        | **Fumadocs UI** under `/lab` (Italian) and `/en/lab` (English): an article per bot on its strategy, one on the arena method, and result pages built from the arena's exported results (JSON in the repo, with seeds, config and engine version). Prerendered, with search; charts are our own SVG, rendered at build                                                                                                                | The research is the point of the project: its results need a page that outlives a notebook, one tap away from the game                                         |
| 18  | RL bot           | **Hybrid** (2026-11-02): the network chooses the card while the deck lasts; once it is empty the exact solver plays, as in PIMC and ISMCTS. A pure network stays a possible later experiment | The last three tricks are already solved, so training spends nothing on them, and the comparison with PIMC, which also ends with the solver, is on equal terms |
| 19  | Network runtime  | **A Rust bot in `core` reads the weights** (2026-11-02): PyTorch trains and exports the weights in a plain format; `py` and `wasm` read the file and hand the bytes to `core`, whose MLP forward pass is hand-written with no dependencies. A test checks Rust against PyTorch on the same inputs. ONNX stays only as an export logged to MLflow. Revises decisions 7 and 12 where they name ONNX | The arena stays one Rust arena with the bot chosen by name, and the site adds a few KB instead of 10–25 MB of onnxruntime-web |
| 20  | Network input    | **`Observation::encode()` in `core`** (2026-11-02): 204 numbers. Five sets of 40 (hand, card on the table, cards played, the opponent's known cards, the face-up briscola), the two players' points over 120, the cards in the deck over 34, whether the player leads. The output is one of 40 cards, illegal ones masked. The order of the tricks and who took which card stay out | One encoding for training, arena and site, with nothing a player at the table does not know; the trick history would only model a style of opponent that keeps changing in self-play |
| 21  | Reward           | **The result only** (2026-11-02): +1 for a win, −1 for a loss, 0 for a draw, at the end of the game after the solver. If training stalls, the first experiment is the result plus a small share of the point margin | It is what the arena measures, and with about 17 choices before the solver the signal is close enough to the choices that caused it |
| 22  | Opponents        | **A pool** (2026-11-02): the current network in about half the games, its last 10 snapshots in about a third, Greedy and Hard in the rest; the network plays both seats. The shares are a starting point, recorded in each run's config. PIMC stays out of training, and PIMC at 10 determinisations joins only if the network stalls at Hard's level | Past snapshots keep it from forgetting how to beat older play, the rule bots show at once if it gets worse against simple play, and PIMC is too slow for millions of games |
| 23  | Environment      | **A vectorised environment in `engine/py`** (2026-11-02): one object holds many games (for example 512); Python passes the network's cards for all of them in one call, and Rust plays them, moves Greedy or Hard where they are the opponent, ends each game with the solver and returns the encoded observations, masks and rewards in bulk. Its cards per second are measured before PPO is written. PettingZoo leaves this milestone. Revises decision 7 where it names PettingZoo | One call per card through Python costs more than the card itself, and PPO needs tens of millions of cards; nobody needs the PettingZoo interface yet |
| 24  | Training runs    | **At night on the Mac, on all cores** (2026-11-02), CPU only, one run per night under `caffeinate` so the Mac does not sleep. An always-on home server was checked and left out: a CPU from 2014 without AVX2, slower in a whole day than the Mac in one night. Revises decision 10 where it names MPS | The Mac is free at night and much faster; a benchmark on two machines was more work than it saves |
| 25  | Training budget  | **No fixed budget** (2026-11-02): start with runs, watch how the network learns, then decide how far to go from what turns out feasible and hard. During a run it plays 1000 duplicate deals against Hard and against PIMC at regular intervals, logged to MLflow | Nobody knows yet how long it needs; a cap set now would be a guess |

## Architecture

```
engine/      Cargo workspace
  core/      rules, state as bitsets (40 cards fit a u64), seeded RNG, observation, bots (search)
  wasm/      wasm-bindgen wrapper for the site
  py/        PyO3 module for the lab
lab/         Python (uv): arena, PPO over the vectorised environment, MLflow logging, notebooks
web/         TanStack Start, static: game UI, bot worker, PWA, Lab pages (Fumadocs)
docs/        rules, and later the arena protocol and bot write-ups
```

The engine is the only place that knows the rules. The lab and the site never reimplement them.

## Milestones

1. **Engine**: rules from [docs/rules.md](docs/rules.md), tests (120 points per game, no duplicate cards, only legal
   moves, recorded reference games), seeded and reproducible.
2. **Arena**: Random, Greedy, Hard; duplicate deals, intervals, rating; runs logged to MLflow; results exported
   as JSON for the Lab pages.
3. **Search**: endgame solver, PIMC, ISMCTS, tuned in the arena. Done on 2026-10-30: PIMC is the champion, and
   ISMCTS at 1000 iterations ties it ([lab/results/ismcts.json](lab/results/ismcts.json)).
4. **Site v1** (epic [#1](https://github.com/MarinCervinschi/briscolab/issues/1), done on 2026-11-01): play the search bots at three or four difficulty levels, PWA, Umami events; the Lab pages with
   the arena results and an article per bot. Online.
5. **RL (v1.1)** (epic [#13](https://github.com/MarinCervinschi/briscolab/issues/13)): the hybrid PPO bot of decisions 18 to 25. In order: `Observation::encode()` and the network
   bot in `core`; the vectorised environment in `engine/py`, with its cards per second measured; PPO in self-play in
   `lab/`, logged to MLflow; the arena. Done means: the arena has run 10 000 duplicate deals between the network
   and PIMC, and the result is in `lab/results/` and in a Lab article, whether the network wins or not. It joins
   the site only if its interval against PIMC is above 50%; first it has to beat Hard.

## Screens (site v1)

1. **Home**: choose a level and start a game. Five levels, each one bot: Easy (Greedy), Medium (Hard), Hard
   (Endgame), Expert (ISMCTS, which can stop at any iteration and so fits a time budget), Champion (PPO, the
   network, added on 2026-11-03 once it beat PIMC in the arena).
2. **Game**: the bot's hand face down, briscola under the deck, points at the end. On a phone, a portrait table
   with your three cards at the thumb, tap to play. On desktop, a wide table with the tricks taken and the cards
   played in view; click or press 1–3 to play. The bot thinks in view: for ISMCTS the hands it imagines for you
   and a bar per card growing with the iterations, for PPO the probability the network gives each card, for Endgame each card's exact result, for Greedy and Hard the
   rule that fired. Whatever would give away the bot's hand is blurred until the player reveals it; the point is
   to watch how the bot reasons, not to beat it at all costs.
3. **Result**: score, a replay of the tricks with the bot's reasoning at every move.
4. **Lab** (Fumadocs, `/lab`): the arena leaderboard with intervals, an article per bot on how it thinks, the
   arena method, and a page per published run.
5. **Settings**: the card back, the theme, animations, fast thinking.
6. **Credits / privacy**: card attribution, the Umami note.

## Open questions

- The result plus a small share of the point margin as the reward (decision 21): the first variant if training
  stalls.
- PIMC at 10 determinisations in the pool (decision 22): if the network stalls at Hard's level.
- A pure network over all 20 tricks (decision 18): a later experiment for the Lab.
- A PettingZoo interface over the environment (decision 23): only if the environment is published for others.
- The network's size: two layers of 256 is the starting guess, tuned in the runs.
