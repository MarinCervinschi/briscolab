---
title: The arena
description: How we measure the bots' strength, and when one bot beats another.
---

Briscola has a lot of luck in it: whoever is dealt the carichi and the briscole starts ahead, whatever the opponent
does. To compare two bots the arena has to take that luck out, and say when a difference is real and when it is
noise.

## Duplicate deals

Every deal is played twice from the same shuffle: in the second game the two bots swap seats. If the cards favour a
seat, they favour each bot once.

A bot's score on a deal is the mean of its two games: 1 for a win (more than 60 points), 0.5 for a 60-60 draw, 0
for a loss. The margin is the mean point difference per game.

Every pair of bots plays the same seeds: in the published runs, deals 0 to 9999. Every game can be replayed exactly
from its seed.

## Intervals

For every pair the arena computes the mean score over 10 000 deals and its 95% interval, by the normal
approximation. A bot **beats** another only when the whole interval of its score lies above 50%, as PIMC's
69.9% ± 0.6 against Hard does. When the interval includes 50%, like ISMCTS's 50.1% ± 0.7 against PIMC, the two bots
cannot be told apart.

The champion changes only this way: watching a few games is not enough.

## Ratings

From the results of every pair the arena computes a Bradley-Terry rating per bot, with Hunter's MM algorithm (2004),
on the Elo scale: a gap of 400 points means 10 to 1 odds, and the mean is 0. The rating puts the bots in a line, but
it is the head-to-head score with its interval that says who beats whom.

## What we publish

Each published run is a JSON file in `lab/results` in the repository, with the bots, the number of deals, the first
seed, the engine version and the commit it was written from. Each file has its page in this Lab, under “Arena runs”,
and anyone can play it again:

```bash
cd lab
uv run briscolab-arena --deals 10000 --out results/ismcts.json
```
