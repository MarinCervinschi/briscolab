Briscolab compares bots that play two-player Briscola, from the simplest, which plays at random, to two search bots
that simulate thousands of games before every card and a neural network trained by playing against itself. Each bot sees only what a player at the table would see: its
hand, the cards played, the briscola, how many cards are left in the deck.

A bot becomes the champion only in the arena: everyone against everyone, 10 000 duplicate deals per pair, and an
advantage counts only when its 95% interval excludes 50%. [How the arena works](/en/lab/arena).

## The bots

| Bot                        | Level on the site | How it chooses its card                                |
| -------------------------- | ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| [Random](/en/lab/random)   | —                 | At random among the legal cards                        |
| [Greedy](/en/lab/greedy)   | Easy              | Wins the current trick when it pays                    |
| [Hard](/en/lab/hard)       | Medium            | Seven rules of an experienced player                   |
| [Endgame](/en/lab/endgame) | Hard              | Hard while the deck lasts, then exact play             |
| [PIMC](/en/lab/pimc)       | —                 | Tries each card on 100 imagined deals                  |
| [ISMCTS](/en/lab/ismcts)   | Expert            | A search tree over 1000 imagined deals                 |
| PPO                        | Champion          | A neural network while the deck lasts, then exact play |
