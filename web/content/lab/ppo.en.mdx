---
title: PPO
description: A neural network trained by playing millions of games against itself. The Champion level.
---

PPO is a neural network that chooses the card while the deck lasts. Once the deck is empty every card is known, and
exact play takes over, as in [Endgame](/en/lab/endgame). The name comes from the algorithm it was trained with,
_proximal policy optimization_ (Schulman and others, 2017).

## What it sees

Before each card the network reads 204 numbers, taken from the player's observation and nothing else:

- five groups of 40 numbers, one per card of the deck: the cards in hand, the cards on the table, the cards played
  in finished tricks, the cards it knows the opponent holds (the face-up briscola, if the opponent drew it) and the
  face-up briscola;
- its own points and the opponent's, over 120;
- the cards left to draw, over 34;
- 1 if it leads the trick.

## How it is built

Three layers of 512 neurons and a last layer with one number per card. The cards it cannot play are dropped; the
others become probabilities, and the network plays the most likely one. Each card takes about 650 000
multiplications. In the browser, in WebAssembly, a move takes less than a millisecond.

## How it trained

Nobody showed it how to play well. It played whole games and learned only from the result: +1 for a win, −1 for a
loss, 0 for a draw.

- **The opponents.** Half the games against itself, a third against its own earlier versions (the last 10, saved
  every 20 updates), the rest against [Greedy](/en/lab/greedy) and [Hard](/en/lab/hard). The earlier versions and
  the rule bots are there so it does not specialise against a single way of playing.
- **The update.** 512 games at a time; every 32 768 cards played the network is updated with PPO, 4 passes in
  batches of 4096. The learning rate starts at 0.0003 and falls to 0 by the end of training.
- **How long.** 340 minutes on a Mac with an M4, 7.4 million games.

During training the network now and then plays 1000 duplicate deals against Hard and against PIMC, always the same
ones. The version that did best against PIMC is kept. Over 1000 deals the interval is about ±2 percentage points, so the curve
wobbles:

| Minutes | Games played | Against Hard | Against PIMC |
| ------: | -----------: | -----------: | -----------: |
|      30 |      684 360 |        61.2% |        46.4% |
|      60 |    1 298 760 |        68.6% |        50.7% |
|     120 |    2 604 360 |        71.9% |        55.7% |
|     180 |    3 909 960 |        74.6% |        56.5% |
|     240 |    5 215 560 |        75.0% |        59.4% |
|     340 |    7 442 760 |        75.6% |        59.6% |

It draws level with PIMC after about an hour. After four hours the curve against PIMC rises by only a few tenths.

## What did not work

Each idea was measured in the arena against the champion network of the time, a smaller network with two layers of
256 neurons. None of them beat it:

| Idea                                                                |  Deals | The champion network's score |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------- | -----: | ---------------------------: |
| PIMC with the network playing in its simulations, 30 imagined deals | 10 000 |           57.1% [56.4, 57.7] |
| The same, 100 imagined deals                                        |   1000 |           54.4% [52.4, 56.5] |
| The network proposes two cards, PIMC chooses between them           |   2000 |     50.4% [49.0, 51.9], draw |
| A critic that also sees the hidden cards (in training only)         | 10 000 |           52.6% [52.0, 53.3] |
| Half the reward from the result, half from the point difference     | 10 000 |           50.8% [50.1, 51.4] |
| Both ideas together, 340 minutes                                    | 10 000 |     50.0% [49.3, 50.6], draw |

On the cards where the network is unsure, PIMC does not choose better than it does, so search on top of the network
does not help. The reward on points brought no more wins. Only a larger network trained for longer worked: three
layers of 512, with the learning rate falling to 0, beats the previous network 51.8% [51.2, 52.5] over 10 000
duplicate deals.

## On the site

It is the Champion level. The network runs in the browser, in a Web Worker, with the same weights as in the arena.
The reasoning shows what it sees, the most active neurons of each layer and the probability it gives each card.
